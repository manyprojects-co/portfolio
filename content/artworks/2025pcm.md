artistStatement: >-
  PCM is a common abbreviation found in UK property listings for rentals priced
  “Per Calendar Month”. *2026PCM* is a video sculpture made to loop
  indefinitely, depicting a single-square-meter bedsit from ingress to eviction.
  Its title inflates to match the current year and looms like an impending bill.
  Aptly so: it is a work about a version of home familiar to exchange students,
  visa-aspirants, and digital nomads (of the weak-passport variety). That home
  is a palimpsest of old tenants and new IKEA orders, lost easily to letting
  agents in a landlord’s market. But for brief, beautiful periods, it is home
  made nevertheless.

technicalText: >-
  In order to capture the real feel of a London new-build, its construction
  process was kept authentic to the locality: the shell was built in the UK,
  with sockets, switches, and a nightlatch from London suppliers, while the
  custom-fit Scandi furniture was factory-fabricated in Qingyuan, China.


  Despite its minuscule footprint, the space is furnished with all the bare
  amenities that a landlord could reasonably upsell, like running water, a
  functional induction stove, and a generous amount of natural light. The room
  was inspired by Xin’s first London flat in Earl’s Court and decorated with
  personal items accrued over multiple moves: a Jellycat plush, some postcards
  from Kew Gardens, and fairy lights from Amazon UK.


  This work is in memory of her first potted plant, Snakey, who was adopted from
  Tesco in 2021 and lived just long enough to star in this film.
