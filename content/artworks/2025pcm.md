artistStatement: >-
  PCM is a common abbreviation found in UK property listings for rentals priced
  “Per Calendar Month”. For a video sculpture made to loop indefinitely
  depicting a single-square-meter bedsit from ingress to eviction, the name
  *2026PCM* looms like an impending bill. Aptly so: it is a work about a version
  of home familiar to exchange students, visa-aspirants, and digital nomads (of
  the weak-passport variety). That home is a palimpsest of old tenants and new
  IKEA orders, lost easily to letting agents in a landlord’s market. But for
  brief, beautiful periods, it is home made nevertheless.


  [YEAR]PCM was produced in collaboration with [Joy
  Chao](https://www.instagram.com/filmperfsobserve/) (cinematography, guest
  performance). The name of this artwork will increment every year as property
  value rises. Its current title is 2026PCM.

technicalText: >-
  Taking fragments from Xin’s first studio in London, the auto-fictional
  installation depicts a London flat measuring just one cubic metre. Fully
  furnished with bare essentials with an IKEA-esque aesthetic, the work captures
  the absurdity of a London home that is way too small for a price that is way
  too high.


  To properly convey the absurdity of a person making do with a tiny flat, the
  space was designed to contain all the bare amenities that a landlord could
  reasonably upsell. That meant sourcing real sockets, latches, switches,
  factory-fabricating custom furniture, and constructing a functional
  kitchenette.
