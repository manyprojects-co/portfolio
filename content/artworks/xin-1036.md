artistStatement: >-
  Artificial intelligence consumes and reproduces human work at a hyperobjective
  scale that is functionally incomprehensible. Xin-1.0.36 was conceived as an
  autoethnographic inquiry into this phenomenon by humanizing it: five years of
  the artist’s personal journal entries (2018-2022) were appended onto the
  system-prompt of a language model and the 36th iteration was then interviewed
  in parallel with the artist herself. The work was expected to evoke immediate
  feelings of discomfort, but the resulting interaction was plausibly and
  empathically human, transforming the piece into an inquiry on the infungible
  value of memory and the duplicitous charisma of contemporary technology.


  The temporal cutoff for selected journal entries was set to November 29, 2022,
  the day prior to ChatGPT’s public release and the subsequent proliferation of
  generative systems across daily life. The final tally of data surpassed half a
  million words in sum. Questions for the resulting model and artist ranged from
  “What’s in your pocket currently?” to “What’s your biggest hope for the world
  in your lifetime?”

technicalTagline: Versions of self
technicalText: >-
  AI can only exist where great human work is available to scrounge, and Xin's
  extensive journals [n] were an apt substrate for a custom-built LLM version of
  her. The work is a recording of two parallel interviews, with the human and
  digital double answering the same questions side-by-side. 


  The digital double is a Gemma3: 12 Billion parameter large language model
  running locally on a Macbook Pro, privately housed within AnythingLLM. This
  model was customized by Retrieval-Augmented Generation referencing a system
  prompt formed through the journals. This system prompt went through 36
  different iterations before landing on a satisfactory model.
