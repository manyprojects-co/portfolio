      Tracing its origins back to [2024](https://agawen.com/art/personhood),
      Personhood (Version 4) debuts a new evolution for XJ’s interactive shadow
      installation at Martha Van Rensselaer Gallery, Cornell University.


      This version of Personhood integrates a proprietary projection-mapping
      tool built from scratch, marking a departure from off-the-shelf projection
      tools and Unity engine mechanics. It features real-time Kinect depth
      sensing and adjustable sensitivity parameters to drive a branching
      animation decision tree.


      The 4th iteration of Personhood was curated by So-Yeon Yoon, and supported
      by [Cornell Council for the Arts](https://cca.cornell.edu/).
  - mediaType: text
    textContent: >-
      Personhood (Iteration 4) relies on state-based branching animation trees
      formed via AI animations that interpolate strategically between pivotal
      keyframes. At every given moment, the artwork “decides” which character
      animation to transition into next. Prior to recent breakthroughs in video
      generation, branching a complex scene into several alternate paths at this
      degree of seamlessness would have been close to impossible.  


      Previous iteration of the interaction used Unity to simulate characters
      casting shadows in the SF Asian Art Museum. Those procedural techniques
      yielded uniquely responsive character animations, but at best they moved
      like NPCs. This iteration foregoes that procedural quality to prioritize
      human fidelity, with a shadow formed in the likenesses of Xin.


      *Curated by So-Yeon Yoon, supported by Cornell Council for the Arts.*
